## Section — The standard, in one paragraph

Body-lg, max 65ch:

Every product we make moves through a quality system designed around three ideas: identify the right specification, follow the procedure, and let any operator stop the line if something looks off. That's it. There is no clever version of quality.

---

## Section — Six commitments, written as verbs

Replaces the current "Striving / Committed / Defined / Empowered / Superior / Dedicated" list. Same six values, said in plain English.

### 1. Improve. Continuously.
We invest in the quality system before we have to. New equipment, new training, new procedures — measured against the products we ship, not the slides we present.

### 2. Comply. Across every level.
cGMP isn't the QA team's job. It's the operator's, the supervisor's, the engineer's, and the executive's.

### 3. Define. Then document.
A culture of accountability requires written procedures. We write them, we follow them, and we update them when the work changes.

### 4. Empower. The person closest to the work.
Operators have the authority to stop a batch. Supervisors have the authority to escalate without permission. That is the only way quality scales.

### 5. Train. Then verify.
Every role has a training matrix. Every cycle has a competency check. Skipping training is not a shortcut — it's a deviation.

### 6. Hold the line. Honestly.
Honesty in deviation reports, in CAPAs, in audits. The quickest way to fix a problem is to write it down.
